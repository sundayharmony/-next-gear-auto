# NJ Tickets on Rental-Fleet Vehicles: Legal Research for Next Gear Auto (Hudson County)

Prepared 2026-11-22. Research notes, not legal advice. Statements paraphrased.

**Tags:** [CONFIRMED-OFFICIAL] = read on a government source (pub.njleg.gov, njcourts.gov, nj.gov, municipal site). [SECONDARY] = Justia/FindLaw/news/blog only. [UNVERIFIED-ASK ATTORNEY] = not found or could not be confirmed.

**Access note:** Direct fetches of pub.njleg.gov statute pages and the njleg bill-status pages were blocked in this session. Current statute text for Title 39 was therefore read on Justia (secondary). Official-source text was obtained for P.L.2007 c.348 (red light pilot) and P.L.2000 c.85 (school bus), plus bill text for S3218.

## Bottom line

1. **Parking:** Real statutory shift mechanism exists. A lessor files a notarized statement (naming the lessee and address) with the municipal court clerk. Statute text is secondary-sourced; the court's prescribed form was NOT found.
2. **Red-light cameras:** No active NJ program. The pilot lapsed 12/16/2014. Its lessor-affidavit provision no longer matters in practice.
3. **Speed cameras:** Still barred. S3218 (school-zone cameras) is only introduced and in committee as of the latest sources found. No enacted law found.
4. **Police-issued moving violations:** The summons belongs to the driver. No lessor "shift" statute needed or found. The owner's job is to give the police/court the renter's identity and to rebut owner presumptions where they exist.
5. A rental-agreement clause making the renter pay does not bind the municipality. It only supports your own recovery against the renter. See "Contract clause" below.

## 4. Police-issued moving violations (summons to driver)

Key distinction: on a parking ticket the vehicle owner is the default defendant, and the lessor statute lets you name the renter. On a moving violation, a police officer normally stops the driver and issues a summons to the person driving. The owner is not the defendant, so there is nothing for the owner to "transfer." The owner's issue arises only when a summons or notice reaches the owner by mail/registration records or the driver was not stopped (e.g., school bus, other owner-presumption statutes).

## 5. Contract clause does not remove owner liability to a municipality

- The parking statute (39:4-139.5) places liability on the owner by default and gives a lessor relief only through the statutory statement process. A private rental-agreement clause (renter pays all fines) is not mentioned as an exemption. [SECONDARY]
- The same statute lets an owner who pays recover from the driver. So the clause matters for reimbursement against the renter (contract/small claims), not for the municipality. [SECONDARY]
- The municipality is not a party to the rental agreement. Whether the agreement may be used as supporting evidence of lessee identity is an evidentiary matter. [UNVERIFIED-ASK ATTORNEY]
- Do not rely on any clause or on the renter's promise to pay to avoid a default judgment, license/registration consequences, or fine escalation.

## 7. Questions for the attorney

1. What is the current form the Administrative Director prescribes for the 39:4-139.5(b) lessor statement (name/number), and do Hudson County courts (Jersey City, Bayonne, Hoboken, Union City, Kearny, North Bergen, Secaucus, etc.) accept one standing form? Is "notarized" strictly required on the current form?
2. Do any courts accept bulk or e-filed lessor statements (NJMCdirect or by email)? Can we file before the court date, and what happens if the court date has passed?
3. Is a rental agreement copy plus driver license enough, or must the statement stand alone? What proof of lessee address should be kept?
4. Is the lessor exemption lost if the vehicle was unauthorized, stolen, or maintenance-related? How should the fleet document maintenance to defend a mechanical-failure claim?
5. If a default judgment is entered before we file, what is the process to reopen (1-year window under 139.8(c)) and does a filed lessor statement cure a default?
6. For moving violations where the owner is summoned or presumed (e.g., 39:4-128.1 school bus), what evidence best rebuts the registered-owner presumption for a rental fleet?
7. Is any lessor-specific statute or court rule available for non-parking Title 39 notices mailed to the owner of record? Is registration suspension under 39:8-71 a risk for unanswered notices on rental cars?
8. Confirm S3218's status on njleg.gov and whether any amendment adds lessor provisions. Is 39:4-103.1 still in force unchanged?
9. Any NJ-authorized camera ticket (red-light or otherwise) still outstanding or pending in a Hudson County municipality?
10. How should rental agreement terms be revised (renter consent to disclose identity to courts, authorization to charge administrative fees, indemnity for fines) so they are enforceable against renters, recognizing they do not bind municipalities?
11. For out-of-state notices (NY, PA, DE, MD), what is our exposure and best process, and can unpaid out-of-state camera tickets affect NJ registration of fleet vehicles?
12. Are fees charged to renters for processing tickets lawful under NJ consumer protection (e.g., TCCWNA) and do the agreement disclosures need changes?
